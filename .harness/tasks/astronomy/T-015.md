## Context

Body rotation was narrowed by the answered questions to constant tilt plus constant rate, but with IAU-style parameterisation (a scalar tilt cannot fix the azimuth of the spin axis) and with W0 so absolute phase is correct. The astronomy package owns the frame conversion; the renderer only maps axes into scene space. Validation is a round-trip/continuity test, not a numerical comparison against an external ephemeris.

## Task

Implement the orientation helper in the astronomy package that converts a registry body's J2000-equatorial IAU orientation constants into a mean-ecliptic-of-date spin axis plus absolute prime-meridian angle at a given instant. Add continuity, round-trip and Earth-obliquity sanity tests. Document the constant-pole simplification and the exclusion of libration.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] poleToEclipticOfDate(bodyOrientation, instant) accepts the IAU-form orientation metadata (poleRA, poleDec in J2000 equatorial degrees, W0, rotationRateDegPerDay, epochJulianDate) and returns the spin-axis direction expressed in mean ecliptic of date coordinates plus the prime-meridian angle at that instant
- [ ] The prime-meridian angle is absolute: W = W0 + rotationRate × (JD − epochJD), normalized, so rotation phase is physically meaningful rather than arbitrary
- [ ] Continuity test: the returned axis and angle vary continuously across instants with no discontinuity at day, year or normalization boundaries
- [ ] Round-trip test: converting the pole to ecliptic of date and back to J2000 equatorial recovers the input to a documented tolerance
- [ ] Earth's returned obliquity-equivalent axis tilt is within a documented tolerance of the known ~23.44°, as a sanity check on the transformation direction
- [ ] The Moon's synchronous rotation is represented by its constant rotation rate only; libration is explicitly out of scope and documented as such
- [ ] No time-varying pole model is implemented and this simplification is documented as a known limitation
- [ ] The helper contains no rendering concepts and returns frame-annotated results
